# Indian Knowledge System Ethics vs. Moral Foundations Theory

**Question asked:** does Moral Foundations Theory (MFT) — the framework chosen for the moral-profile ML target — match how morality is understood in the Indian Knowledge System (IKS)?

**Short answer:** partly. MFT's academic lineage runs directly through India, so it isn't a foreign import — but the specific foundations don't weight the same way, and that has concrete consequences for story design and instrument choice below.

---

## 1. MFT didn't skip India — it partly came from India

Jonathan Haidt built Moral Foundations Theory as a direct extension of anthropologist **Richard Shweder's "Big Three" ethics**: Autonomy, Community, Divinity. Shweder derived the Big Three from decades of fieldwork, much of it in **Bhubaneswar, Orissa — a Hindu temple town** — comparing moral reasoning across Brahmin families, Dalit ("Untouchable") families, and a Judeo-Christian comparison sample in Chicago ([Shweder, Dept. of Comparative Human Development, UChicago](https://humdev.uchicago.edu/directory/richard-shweder)).

The mapping from Shweder's ethics to Haidt's foundations is direct and documented:

| Shweder's ethic | → | MFT foundation(s) |
|---|---|---|
| Autonomy | → | Care/Harm, Fairness/Cheating |
| Community | → | Loyalty/Betrayal, Authority/Subversion |
| Divinity | → | Sanctity/Purity |

([source](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/uniting-theories-of-morality-religion-and-social-interaction-grid))

One of MFT's two original field sites was India. It is not a Western framework being imposed on Indian participants.

---

## 2. Indian Knowledge System ethics, briefly

Vedic/Hindu-lineage IKS ethics centers on the **Purusharthas** — four aims of life:

| Aim | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Dharma | righteousness, duty |
| Artha | prosperity, economic value |
| Kama | pleasure, psychological value |
| Moksha | liberation, spiritual self-realisation |

Dharma holds primacy over the other three — ancient texts (Gautama Dharmashastra, Apastamba Dharmasutra, Yajnavalkya Smriti) insist Dharma must guide and restrain the pursuit of wealth and pleasure ([source](https://www.academia.edu/129382409/Indian_Knowledge_System_IKS_A_Blueprint_of_the_Purushartha_Scheme_for_Human_Flourishing)).

Dharma is expressed through:
- **Varnashrama** — duties tied to social role (varna) and life stage (ashrama)
- **Svadharma** — *personal* duty, defined by one's specific role and context, distinct from *Sanatana Dharma*, the universal eternal moral order ([source](https://philosophy.institute/indian-philosophy/varnashrama-dharma-ancient-social-order-india/))

Key point: Svadharma is **relationally defined** — duty flows from role and context, not from a prior, autonomous individual whose rights duty might infringe on.

## 3. Mapping — my synthesis, not a cited study

I searched specifically for an existing academic paper mapping MFT onto Dharma directly and found none. The table below is my own reasoning from the two separate literatures above, not a citable direct comparison.

| MFT foundation | Nearest Dharmic concept | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| Care/Harm | Ahimsa (non-violence) | Reasonable |
| Fairness/Cheating | *no clean equivalent — Karma is cosmic/causal, not interpersonal reciprocity* | Weak |
| Loyalty/Betrayal | Duties to family, community, group | Strong |
| Authority/Subversion | Varnashrama role-hierarchy, respect for elders/tradition | Strong |
| Sanctity/Purity | Ritual purity, caste-linked purity gradations | Strong |

**Pattern:** Care and Fairness — MFT's two *individualizing* foundations, about protecting an autonomous individual's interests — are the weakest fits. Loyalty, Authority, and Sanctity — the *binding* foundations, about role, hierarchy, and group cohesion — map far more directly onto Dharma.

Not a coincidence. MFT's individualizing/binding split assumes a **rights-bearing individual as the basic moral unit**. Dharmic ethics doesn't have that unit in the same way — Svadharma is defined by role and context, not by a self whose prior rights duty might infringe on. Some Indian-philosophy scholars would argue the individualizing/binding axis itself is a culturally specific cut, not a natural one.

## 4. This has been measured, not just theorized

**MFQ-2** (the newer, better-validated version of the Moral Foundations Questionnaire) was specifically calibrated using Indian samples:
- Study 1b: 90 candidate items tested across India, US, Iran
- Study 3: 1,410 participants across US, India, Canada

Reliability was strong (omega 0.73–0.95) and the five-factor structure held up — **the instrument itself doesn't break in an Indian sample** ([source](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12144-025-07630-4)).

But the broader cross-cultural MFT literature consistently finds: **Western samples emphasize Care and Fairness over the binding foundations; Eastern samples show a more balanced profile across all five** ([source](https://www.psypost.org/decoding-morality-across-cultures-insights-from-a-mega-study-of-moral-foundations-theory/)).

Structure survives. Weighting shifts. And it shifts in exactly the direction the Dharma material predicts.

---

## 5. Caveat: "Indian Knowledge System" is not one thing

Everything above is Vedic/Hindu-lineage ethics specifically. IKS as a broader category also includes:
- **Buddhist ethics** — the Eightfold Path, its own precept structure
- **Jain ethics** — Ahimsa taken further, anekantavada (non-absolutism)
- **Secular Niti literature** — Chanakya, the Panchatantra, Thirukkural

None of these map onto Dharma/Varnashrama the same way, and none were researched for this document. Don't present "the Indian view" as singular in any writeup built on this file.

---

## 6. Consequences for this project

1. **Use MFQ-2, not the older MFQ-30/MFQ-1**, as the validation questionnaire. It's the version actually tested on Indian participants; the older one wasn't built with this in mind.
2. **Don't over-load the story set with Care/Fairness-style dilemmas** — the classic Western trolley-problem shape. If binding foundations (Loyalty, Authority, Sanctity) carry more real weight in the participant pool, a story set skewed toward individualizing dilemmas under-elicits the foundations most likely to actually distinguish people.
3. **Expect the MFQ-2 cross-check to run warmer on some foundations than others.** This is a literature-supported pattern, not a measurement bug if it shows up in results.
